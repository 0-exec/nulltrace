Emotet

Type: Banking Trojan / Malware Loader

Overview

Emotet is a modular malware family first identified in 2014. It originally operated as a banking trojan designed to steal financial information. Over time, it evolved into a sophisticated malware distribution platform. Its infrastructure was also used to distribute additional malware operated by other cybercriminal groups.

Technical Details

Emotet commonly spreads through phishing emails containing malicious attachments or links. Once a victim executes the malicious file, Emotet can establish persistence, communicate with command-and-control (C2) infrastructure and download additional malicious components.

Historically observed capabilities include:

  • Credential and financial information theft
  • Email address and contact collection
  • Command-and-control communication
  • Persistence through Windows registry entries
  • Distribution of additional malware
  • Use of compromised systems as part of a botnet

Historical Timeline

  • 2014: First identified as a banking trojan.
  • 2017: Expanded into a malware distribution platform.
  • January 2021: International law enforcement operation disrupted its infrastructure.
  • November 2021: Emotet activity resumed.

Impact

Emotet has been associated with significant cyber incidents worldwide. Its ability to distribute additional malware made it particularly dangerous. Infected systems could become entry points for further attacks, including ransomware infections. In January 2021, the US Department of Justice reported that approximately 1.6 million computers worldwide appeared to have been infected during the period covered by its investigation.

Research Notes

When investigating Emotet samples, analysts can examine file hashes, PE metadata, imported functions, embedded strings and suspicious network indicators. These characteristics can help identify a sample and provide an initial understanding of its potential functionality.

Static analysis should be performed before considering controlled dynamic analysis. Suspicious samples should never be executed directly on a normal workstation.
