QakBot

Type: Banking Trojan / Malware Loader

Overview

QakBot, also known as Qbot, QuackBot or Pinkslipbot, is a Windows malware family active since at least 2008. Originally developed as a banking trojan, it was designed to steal financial information and account credentials from compromised systems. Over time, QakBot evolved into a modular malware platform and became an important part of the cybercriminal ecosystem. It was frequently used to deliver additional malware, including ransomware.

Technical Details

QakBot was commonly distributed through phishing emails containing malicious attachments or links. Some campaigns used email thread hijacking, where attackers replied to existing email conversations using stolen messages. This made malicious messages appear more convincing to recipients. After execution, QakBot could establish communication with command-and-control (C2) infrastructure, collect sensitive information and download additional malicious components.

Its observed capabilities included:

  • Banking credential and password theft
  • Collection of browser data and email information
  • Communication with remote C2 servers
  • Persistence through Windows Registry modifications
  • Process injection and execution of malicious payloads
  • Discovery of systems and network resources
  • Delivery of additional malware and ransomware
  • Participation in a remotely controlled botnet

Distribution and Infection

QakBot campaigns frequently relied on social engineering to convince users to open malicious attachments or follow harmful links. Once executed, the malware could collect information about the compromised system, communicate with attacker-controlled infrastructure and receive instructions or additional payloads. In some cases, compromised systems were used as an entry point for further activity within organizational networks.

Historical Timeline

  • 2008: QakBot activity is documented as an established banking malware family.
  • Following years: The malware evolves with additional information theft and malware delivery capabilities.
  • August 25, 2023: The FBI and international partners execute a coordinated operation to disrupt QakBot infrastructure.
  • August 29, 2023: The U.S. Department of Justice publicly announces the international takedown.

Impact

QakBot was associated with thousands of malware infections and numerous cybercrime operations worldwide. According to the U.S. Department of Justice, the FBI identified more than 700,000 infected computers globally during its investigation, including more than 200,000 in the United States. On August 25, 2023, an international law enforcement operation disrupted QakBot's command-and-control infrastructure. The FBI redirected malicious traffic and delivered an uninstaller to infected computers. The operation disrupted the botnet, but it did not automatically remove other malware or ransomware that may already have been installed on affected systems.

Research Notes

QakBot is a useful case study for understanding modular malware, credential theft and botnet operations. During static and dynamic analysis, researchers can examine PE metadata, imported functions, embedded strings, suspicious Registry references and indicators of command-and-control communication. During behavioral analysis, useful areas to investigate include process injection, persistence, network activity, email artifacts and attempts to discover other systems on the network. The MITRE ATT&CK entry provides a useful reference for mapping observed behaviors to documented adversary techniques. Malware samples should only be handled in an isolated analysis environment.
